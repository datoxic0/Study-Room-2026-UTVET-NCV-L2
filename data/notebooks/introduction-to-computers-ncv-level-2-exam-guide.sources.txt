Section 1: Fill in the Missing Parts (5 Marks)
This section will test your fundamental knowledge of data representation sizes and basic computer classifications.
1. Data Storage Sizes
You must know the exact binary weight of different storage terminologies.
Bit: The smallest unit of data in a computer, representing a single binary digit (either a 0 or a 1).
Nibble: A grouping of exactly 4 bits.
Byte: A grouping of exactly 8 bits.
Word: The natural unit of data used by a particular processor design (typically 16, 32, or 64 bits depending on the system architecture).
2. Types of Computers
Be prepared to fill in blanks regarding the three primary classifications of computers:
Analogue Computers: Process continuous, variable physical quantities (e.g., voltage, temperature) rather than discrete values.
Digital Computers: Process data in discrete, discrete binary forms (0s and 1s). This is the standard modern computer.
Hybrid Computers: Combine features of both analogue and digital computers, often taking continuous analogue inputs and converting them into digital signals for processing.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Section 2: Short Questions & Definitions (15 Marks)
Expect to define core concepts that govern how computers process information electrically.
Binary Logic: A system of mathematical logic that uses two discrete states to solve logical operations and represent variables. These states are commonly represented as "Off" or "Lo" (0) and "On" or "Hi" (1).
Logic Gate: A physical electronic device implementing a Boolean function. It performs a logical operation on one or more binary inputs to produce a single binary output.
Switching Logic Circuit: The electrical equivalent of a logic gate, demonstrating how switches (representing inputs) can be wired in series or parallel to control a load, such as a lamp (representing the output).
Boolean Algebra: The branch of algebra in which the values of the variables are the truth values true and false, usually denoted 1 and 0 respectively.

Digital Logic Primer: Foundations of
Computer Architecture
1. Introduction to the Digital Language
In the field of computer architecture, we define binary logic as the mathematical
and physical framework used to represent and manipulate information through two
distinct states. Unlike the continuous signals found in nature, a digital quantity
consists of a discrete set of values. This discrete nature allows for the precise, reliable
transmission of data across a computer's internal circuitry.
The core of this communication is the binary digit, or "bit." As instructors, we represent
these physical states using symbolic notation to simplify circuit design:
Key Concept: Binary Representation
 0: Represents an electrical state that is "Off" or "Lo" (Low voltage).
 1: Represents an electrical state that is "On" or "Hi" (High voltage).
This distinguishes modern systems from analogue computers, which process
continuous physical quantities. According to foundational technical standards (such
as NCV Level 2), analogue signals are characterized by their rise time, fall time, and
amplitude. Because these signals vary continuously, they suffer from two primary
disadvantages:
1. Noise Sensitivity: Even minor fluctuations in amplitude can alter the data,
leading to errors.
2. Signal Degradation: The inherent characteristics of pulses (rise and fall times)
make analogue signals less reliable for complex processing compared to the
binary "all-or-nothing" threshold.
These discrete values are not merely abstract numbers; they are processed and
transformed by hardware components known as logic gates.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. The Building Blocks: Common Logic Gates
Logic gates are the physical switches that execute Boolean algebra. The most
essential operators in digital expressions are AND and OR.

7. System Maintenance, Security, and
Troubleshooting
Preventative Maintenance
Proactive care involves hardware dusting, software updates, storage management
(clearing temp files), and data backups. A critical tool is Scan Disk, which is used to
examine the disk for logical errors and bad sectors.
Cybersecurity Protocols
Protecting digital assets requires four primary strategies:
1. Installing and updating Anti-virus software.
2. Implementing a Firewall (a barrier that blocks unauthorized network access).
3. Enforcing strong password policies.
4. Scanning all removable media (USB drives) before use.
Advanced Troubleshooting
Troubleshooting is categorized into three types: Hardware, Software, and Network.
CPU Replacement Procedure:
 Step 1: Power down and disconnect the system from the mains.
 Step 2: Open the system unit and locate the CPU socket on the motherboard.
 Step 3: Remove the cooling fan/heatsink assembly.
 Step 4: Release the socket lever, remove the old CPU, and insert the
replacement.
 Step 5: Reapply thermal paste, reattach the cooling system, and close the
unit.
Robot Controller Overheating (Causes & Solutions):
 Causes: Dust accumulation in the controller vents, failure of cooling fans, or
insufficient ambient ventilation.
 Solutions: Clean internal components with compressed air, replace faulty
fans, and ensure the controller is relocated to a well-ventilated area.
