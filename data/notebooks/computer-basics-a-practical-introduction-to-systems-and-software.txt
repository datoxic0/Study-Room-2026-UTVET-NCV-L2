Explain this topic in more detail.

In Boolean Algebra, the addition symbol (+) represents the OR logic gate
1
. Therefore, the Commutative Property for addition (A+B=B+A) simply means that the order of your inputs does not matter when using an OR operator
1
.
To understand this in more detail, it helps to look at how an OR gate functions and why this property is practically useful:
The OR Gate Connection: An OR gate takes multiple binary inputs (like A and B) and will output a 1 (True or "on") if at least one of those inputs is a 1
2
3
. Because of the Commutative Property, the hardware does not care which input comes first; checking if A is on OR B is on yields the exact same logical result as checking if B is on OR A is on
1
.
Simplifying Circuits: Hardware architects use the Commutative Property alongside other Boolean laws (such as the Associative, Identity, Complement, and Null properties) to mathematically simplify complex logical expressions, like Sum of Products (SOP) or Product of Sums (POS)
4
5
.
The Real-World Impact: By using these rules to simplify logical expressions, architects can reduce the total number of physical logic gates (transistors) needed to build a circuit
4
. This optimization is crucial because using fewer gates directly lowers manufacturing costs, reduces the amount of heat the hardware dissipates, and increases the speed at which the CPU can process instructions
4
.
