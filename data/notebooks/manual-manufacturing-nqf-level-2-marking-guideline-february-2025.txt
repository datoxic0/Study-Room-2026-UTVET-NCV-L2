Wednesday, 25 March

To master tomorrow’s NQF Level 2 Manual Manufacturing practical drawing and theory exam, you must focus entirely on SANS 10111 standards, precise geometric construction, and the strict procedural sequencing required to translate a drawing into a physical part.




Here is your high-speed, advanced study guide designed to secure maximum marks:





Geometric Construction: Bisecting a Line (Practical)
Examiners frequently test your ability to use a compass for exact geometric layouts rather than relying on a ruler. You must be able to perform and describe the bisection of a line (e.g., a 7 cm line) using active verbs. Memorize this exact 5-step sequence:





Draw a horizontal base line exactly 7 cm long and label the ends A and B.




Position the compass point on end A and set the radius to greater than half the line length (e.g., 4.5 cm).




Scribe an arc above and an arc below the geometric center of the line.




Repeat the process by placing the compass point on end B without changing the radius, scribing arcs that intersect the first two.




Join the two intersection points with a perpendicular vertical line using a straight edge to perfectly bisect the line.





SANS 10111 Dimensioning Rules (Theory & Practical)
Marks are heavily deducted for sloppy or non-standard dimensioning. Apply these strict rules to all your drawings:





Extension Lines: You must leave a visible 2 mm gap between the drawn object and the start of your extension lines. The extension line must also extend exactly 2 mm past the last dimension line.




Symbols: Numerical values for diameters must always be preceded by the $\emptyset$ symbol, and numerical values for radii must be preceded by an R. Leader lines used for circles or arcs must be radial.




Assumed Angles: Show angles as right angles, which are assumed to be 90 degrees unless otherwise specified.




Clarity: Avoid crossed dimension lines at all costs, and align/group dimensions to promote clarity and a uniform appearance.





Welding Symbols & Section Views (Theory)
You must be able to interpret and draw the standardized welding symbols on a reference line.





Reference Line vs. Arrow: The arrow indicates the joint, and the symbol sits on the continuous reference line.




Square Butt Weld: Represented by two parallel vertical lines.




Single-V Butt Weld: Represented by a 'V' shape.




Double-V Butt Weld: Represented by an 'X' shape (a V on top of an inverted V).




Fillet Weld: Represented by a right-angled triangle.





The Procedural Work Plan: The "Hammer Head" (Theory)
A major theoretical component of the drawing exam often involves writing a procedural work plan based on a provided engineering drawing (like a mild steel hammer head). Examiners look for a logical sequence. Memorize this 10-step professional sequence for manufacturing a hammer head from a drawing:





Cut the mild-steel stock to size and deburr the edges.




File the head square to size. (Crucial step: Do not skip squaring the ends before marking).




Mark the workpiece exactly as per the drawing.




File the required chamfers and bevels.




Cut the wedge and file it to size.




Drill the 12 mm hole exactly as per the drawing.




File the slot to size.




Punch the required name or identification onto the hammer.




Clean and polish the workpiece.




Harden and polish the final product.




Exam Strategy Tip: When drawing, ensure your pencil lines are sharp and distinct. Hidden detail lines (dashed) and center lines (chain-dotted) must be strictly distinguished from your thick, continuous object outlines. Keep your tools clean and double-check your Vernier caliper calculations by multiplying the Vernier scale line by 0.02 mm before adding it to the main scale.
