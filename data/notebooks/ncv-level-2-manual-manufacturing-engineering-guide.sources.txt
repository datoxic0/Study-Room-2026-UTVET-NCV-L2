SECTION 1: Topic 3 – Hand & Power Tools (80% Exam Weight)
Unit 1: Measuring, Marking, and Checking (Metrology)
Before any metal is cut or formed, it must be precisely measured and marked out. Errors here propagate through the entire manufacturing process.
1. Precision Measuring Instruments
Vernier Caliper: Used for measuring external dimensions, internal dimensions, and depth.
Key Component: The Vernier scale provides fractional readings down to 0.02\text{ mm} or 0.05\text{ mm}.
Reading Principle: Read the main scale millimeters to the left of the Vernier scale zero, then find the line on the Vernier scale that aligns perfectly with a line on the main scale.
Outside Micrometer: Used for highly precise external measurements (accuracy typically up to 0.01\text{ mm}).
Key Component: The ratchet stop ensures uniform pressure is applied during measurement, preventing over-tightening and distorted readings.
Sleeve & Thimble: The sleeve provides whole and half-millimeter marks; the rotating thimble provides hundredths of a millimeter.
2. Marking-Out Tools & Materials
Layout Dye (Engineers' Blue): Applied to the metal surface to make scribed lines clearly visible.
Scriber: A hardened steel or tungsten-carbide tipped tool used to scratch fine, precise lines onto the metal surface.
Engineers' Try Square: Used to check and lay out lines at exactly 90^\circ to a datum (reference) edge.
Punches:
Prick Punch (30^\circ angle point): Used to produce small indentations along scribed lines to make them permanently visible.
Center Punch (90^\circ angle point): Used to create a deep indentation at the center point of a hole location to guide and seat a drill bit, preventing it from walking.
Unit 2: Cutting and Forming Operations
Once marked, raw stock material must be separated and shaped to dimension.
1. Hand Cutting (Hacksawing & Filing)
Hacksaw Blade Selection: Chosen based on the material thickness and hardness. Measured in Teeth Per Inch (TPI).
Rule of Thumb: A minimum of 2 to 3 teeth must be in contact with the material cross-section at all times to prevent the teeth from snagging and stripping.
Thin materials (e.g., sheet metal / thin wall tubing): Require high TPI (24 to 32 TPI).
Thick materials (e.g., mild steel bar stock): Require lower TPI (14 to 18 TPI) for efficient chip clearance.
Operation: Directional teeth must point forward (away from the handle). Cutting pressure is applied only on the forward stroke; pressure is released on the return stroke.
Filing Operations:
Cross-filing: Moving the file across the work surface to remove material rapidly and flatten surfaces.
Draw-filing: Grasping the file at both ends and moving it along the length of the workpiece to produce a smooth, fine surface finish.
File Grades: Bastard (coarse material removal), Second-cut (medium shaping), Smooth (finishing).
2. Forming and Bending
Mechanical Bending: Forcing metal beyond its yield point to achieve a permanent deformation (like the curves/bends of a G-strap).
Springback Factor: Metal has natural elasticity. When bent, it will slightly unbend when released. To compensate, components must be slightly over-bent to achieve the final required angle.
Bend Allowance: The amount of material required to form the bend, calculated along the neutral axis (the zone in the metal thickness that neither expands nor compresses during a bend).
Unit 3: Joining, Threading, and Assembly
This unit focuses on how components are mechanically fastened or threaded together.
1. Internal Thread Cutting (Tapping)
To create internal threads inside a drilled hole (e.g., where the clamping bolt screws into a bracket/strap):
Tap Drill Size (TDS) Calculation: The hole drilled must be smaller than the nominal outer diameter of the bolt so that material remains for the tap to cut threads into.
The Three-Tap Set (Hand Taps):
Taper Tap (First Tap): Has a pronounced lead chamfer (8–10 threads tapered). It easily aligns and starts the thread square to the hole.
Intermediate/Second Tap: Has fewer tapered threads (3–5 threads). It deepens the threads started by the taper tap.
Bottoming/Plug Tap (Third Tap): Has almost no taper (1–2 threads). It cuts full-depth threads all the way to the bottom of a blind hole.
Operational Technique: Turn the tap wrench 1/2 to 1 full turn clockwise to cut, then reverse 1/4 to 1/2 turn counter-clockwise to break the chip. Use cutting fluid to lubricate and clear debris.
2. External Thread Cutting (Threading with Dies)
The Button Die: Placed in a die stock, it is used to cut external threads onto a solid round bar (e.g., making a custom threaded bolt or clamping rod).
Preparation: Chamfer/bevel the end of the rod at 45^\circ before starting to allow the die to catch and center smoothly.
SECTION 2: Topic 1 – Safety and Health Care (10% Exam Weight)
Do not drop easy marks here. Expect questions on risk mitigation and workshop protocols.
1. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Matrix
Drilling/Grinding: Safety goggles/face shield (high-velocity flying chip hazard).
Handling Metal Stock: Heavy-duty leather rigging gloves (sharp edges, burrs, thermal burn hazards). Note: Never wear gloves while operating rotating machinery like a drill press or lathe to prevent entanglement hazards.
General Workshop Floor: Steel-toe safety boots (drop hazards, heavy metal plates) and flame-retardant overalls.
2. Workshop Hazard Identification & Controls
Housekeeping (5S Principles): Keeping walkways clear of oil slicks, metal shavings (swarf), and tripped cables.
Machine Guarding: Ensuring interlocks and physical barriers are intact over belts, pulleys, and gears before powering up equipment.
Isolation Procedures (Lockout/Tagout): Disconnecting primary power sources and locking the switchboard before performing any tool changes or maintenance on powered machinery.
